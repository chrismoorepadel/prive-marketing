# Homepage Copy — V2 Proposal

*Proposed, not written into the codebase. Per `overhaul-plan.md`: copy goes through review first.*

---

## The style, derived from the paragraph that worked

> Privé Padel builds the places where the game and good travel meet — our own clubs in Montauk and Miami, and a curated collection of hotels and resorts across five continents. Privé Passport is the membership that opens all of it: one annual membership, member rates and privileges wherever you stay, and a padel court waiting when you arrive.

Six things it does. These are the rules for everything below.

1. **A plain verb, early.** "Builds the places." The brand *does* something before it claims anything.
2. **Concrete nouns carry the weight.** Montauk, Miami, five continents. Not "signature," "refined," "curated luxury."
3. **The relationship is stated.** Privé Padel is the thing; Privé Passport opens it. A reader learns the architecture without being taught a taxonomy.
4. **It lands on physical experience.** "A padel court waiting when you arrive." Not a benefit label — a moment.
5. **No internal vocabulary.** It never says "Padel Destinations" or "four offerings."
6. **No clause repeats the one before it.** Every phrase adds.

**The homepage's dominant copy problem is #6.** Most qualifier lines restate their own headline instead of adding to it. The second-biggest is #5: architecture names are used as customer-facing labels.

---

## 1. Offerings intro — *worth changing*

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | `MEMBER ACCESS INCLUDES` / **One membership.** *Four ways to play.* |
| Proposed | `WHAT YOUR PASSPORT OPENS` / **One membership.** *Four doors it opens.* |

"Four ways to play" announces the org chart. "Four doors it opens" carries the same structure but uses the passport metaphor the brand already owns — and it echoes "opens all of it" from the anchor paragraph, so the two sections rhyme.

*Alternative if "doors" feels laboured:* **One membership.** *Everywhere it takes you.*

---

## 2. The four qualifier lines — *the highest-leverage change on the page*

Each headline is fine. Each qualifier beneath it restates the headline instead of adding information. Rewriting these four lines does more than any other change here.

**Padel Destinations** — headline stays *40+ destinations to stay and play.*
> Now: Preferred benefits at top resorts where padel is on property.
> **Proposed: Book the room and the court is already there — with member rates, upgrades, and resort credits at every property.**

**Padel Experiences** — headline stays *Curated retreats.*
> Now: All-inclusive itineraries built around the game and the place.
> **Proposed: Small groups, a dedicated pro, every meal and excursion handled. Members save 20% and are invited first.**

**Padel Club Partners** — headline stays *Partner clubs in major cities.*
> Now: Partner clubs ready in the cities you travel to most.
> **Proposed: Los Angeles, New York, London, Hong Kong — a court held for you when you're in town for something else.**

Naming the cities is the whole point. "The cities you travel to most" asks the reader to imagine; four city names tell them.

**Privé Locations** — headline stays *Montauk · Miami.*
> Now: Our flagship venues, in two of America's signature places.
> **Proposed: The two clubs we run ourselves. Miami is open now in Coral Gables; Montauk returns Spring 2027.**

This one earns its place twice: "signature places" is an adjective doing no work, and since the venue status labels came out of the nav, this is where a reader now learns Montauk is coming and Miami is open.

## 3. "Preferred" appears five times in the benefits list — *worth changing*

Preferred stays · Preferred room rates · Preferred court access · Preferred member rates · Preferred court pricing.

It's hotel-trade language that tells a member nothing about what they get. The landing page already solved this — *"Preferred room rates — up to 20% off."* Same fix here:

| Now | Proposed |
|---|---|
| Preferred stays at over 40 luxury hotels & resorts with padel courts | Member rates at 40+ hotels and resorts, each with a padel court |
| Preferred room rates | Room rates up to 20% below published |
| Room upgrade upon availability | A better room when one is free |
| Early check-in and late check-out, upon availability | Early arrivals and unhurried departures |
| Resort, restaurant, and spa credits | Resort, dining, and spa credits — typically $100–200 a stay |
| Preferred court access | Court time reserved before you land |
| Curated retreats — first access and preferred pricing | Retreats at 20% off, with the first invitation |
| Preferred court pricing, full club access | Full club access at member court rates |
| Preferred member rates | Member pricing every visit |

Numbers where you have them ($100–200, 20%) do more than any adjective. Confirm the credit range before it ships.

## 4. Worldview moment — *worth changing*

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | *A life played well.* / Privé Padel is our home. Privé Passport is how it travels **with us.** |
| Proposed | *A life played well.* / Privé Padel is where the game lives. Privé Passport is how it travels **with you.** |

The current line is the company talking to itself — "our home," "travels with us." The reader isn't in the sentence. One pronoun change puts them there, and "where the game lives" is more concrete than "our home."

---

## 5. Closing CTA — *worth changing*

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | *Play the world.* / Your Passport is ready. From $595 a year. |
| Proposed | *Play the world.* / One membership, from $595 a year. Cancel any time. |

"Your Passport is ready" assumes a purchase that hasn't happened. Replacing it with the terms adds the one piece of risk reversal the homepage never gives — and the FAQ answer on the landing page shows this is the objection that actually blocks people.

## 6. Featured Destinations — *optional*

| | |
|---|---|
| Now | `Featured Destinations` / **Selected from** *the network.* |
| Proposed | `The Collection` / **Forty-three places** *to land.* |

"Selected from the network" is procedural. "Forty-three places to land" is a number and an image. Lower priority than the above — the current line isn't wrong, just quiet.

## Leave alone

- **Hero** — already in the new voice. One flag below.
- **Brand anchor paragraph** — the reference text.
- **"Choose your Passport."** and its sub-line — clean, and the sub does real work.
- **"Why they joined."** — good.
- **Testimonials** — real member words. Don't edit what people said.
- **Miami and Montauk body copy** — already written in this register.
- **Footer** — taxonomy labels are correct in navigation; that's what they're for.

---

## Three things to resolve before any of this ships

1. **43 or 40+?** The page says "40+ destinations" twice, "View All 43" twice, and the dataset holds 43. Pick one and use it everywhere. *Recommendation: say 43.* A specific number is more credible than a rounded one, and you have the properties to back it.

2. **"The world's first Padel Passport"** is a factual claim in the hero, not a mood. Worth being comfortable defending it before it sits at the top of the site.

3. **"neighbourhood"** in the Miami paragraph is British spelling — my error, and wrong for a US brand. Should be *neighborhood*. Small, but it's on a page about a Florida club.

*(An earlier note here flagged a per-month / per-year conflict in the venue allowance. **Per month** is correct; every surface states it that way.)*
